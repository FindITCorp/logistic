## The Problem

Panama's 50,000+ micro-importers pay **$400–800/m³** to ship goods from China — 4–8x the true cost.

- **Real carrier cost:** $80–100/m³ for consolidated LCL shipping
- **What small importers pay:** $400–800/m³ via couriers and freight agents
- **The gap:** $250–700/m³ in margin captured by intermediaries, not the importer

Why? Small importers can't fill a container alone. Carriers only offer bulk rates at 20+ m³. The infrastructure to group small shippers together and distribute savings fairly has not existed — until now.

**Market context:**
- China exported **$12.77 billion** to Panama in 2024 (TradingEconomics)
- Panama imported from China **$1.76 billion** in 2023 (OEC World)
- Panama's Colón Free Zone is the **2nd largest duty-free trade zone in the world** (after Hong Kong)
- Majority of Panama's micro-importers source consumer goods, clothing, electronics, and hardware from Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and Yiwu

---

## The Solution

**FINDIT Logistic is a dynamic price pooling platform for Panama micro-importers.**

- **Pool:** We group 6–15 small shippers (1–5 m³ each) into a 10-day window
- **Negotiate:** When the pool reaches 6+ m³, we lock a consolidated LCL rate with our Chinese forwarder partner at $80–90/m³
- **Distribute:** Savings are distributed transparently — importers who commit early get more savings, those who join late contribute more to FINDIT's margin
- **Ship:** Every 15 days, cargo moves from Guangzhou/Shenzhen to Colón, Panama

**The pricing engine:**

| Volume of Pool | Carrier Cost | Client Savings vs. $100 reference |
|---|---|---|
| 5–15 m³ | $90/m³ | $10/m³ |
| 15–20 m³ | $85/m³ | $15/m³ |
| 20+ m³ | $80/m³ | $20/m³ |

**Day-of-entry pricing:** Importers who enter the pool on Day 1 get 90% of the savings. Day 5 = 50%. Day 9 = 10%. FINDIT captures the remainder as margin. This incentivizes early commitment and rewards planning.

## Why Now / Why Panama

1. **Panama Canal position** — All China–West Coast South America cargo transits Panama. Colón is already the logistics hub. FINDIT is not building new infrastructure — it's organizing what's already there.

2. **Post-pandemic import surge** — Panama's micro-importer base grew significantly as e-commerce and direct-from-China sourcing became mainstream for small businesses.

3. **No direct competitor** — No platform in Panama currently pools micro-importers for LCL rate optimization. Traditional freight forwarders serve the top of the market; couriers (DHL, FedEx) serve the bottom. FINDIT owns the gap in the middle.

4. **Replicable model** — The same model applies to Costa Rica, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru — all served by Panama Canal routing. Panama is the beachhead.
